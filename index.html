How I Think About AI Programs

Open Question 01
How do we know AI output is accurate? How do we trust it?
Three approaches I use: (1) LLM-as-judge — route output to a second model for independent evaluation; (2) self-check — prompt the original model to critique its own response; (3) SME review loop — route domain-specific output to the relevant team before publishing. Trust isn't binary — it's a layered system you design deliberately.
Open Question 02
How and where do we actually deploy something?
Enterprise tooling constraints are real. I've shipped dashboards to GitHub Pages when internal infrastructure was inaccessible, used Slack as a lightweight deployment surface, and navigated compliance and security review cycles. Knowing how to ship within constraints — not just in an ideal environment — is where real TPM leverage lives.
Principle 01
AI-to-AI collaboration as a design pattern
The most powerful AI workflows aren't single-model — they're pipelines. I design systems that route tasks to the right model: Claude for reasoning and refinement, Gemini for Google Workspace integration, NotebookLM for audio synthesis. Orchestrating models well is itself a product manager skill, not a research skill.
Principle 02
Demonstrating AI fluency means shipping AI tools
Anyone can describe what an LLM can do. I'd rather build the dashboard that eliminates the status meeting, the bot that surfaces information before anyone asks, the proofreading skill that encodes institutional standards as code. Operational AI fluency is measured in tools shipped, not frameworks cited.
